# Product Backlog Refinement

Use this reference when the user wants help preparing, improving, or diagnosing Product Backlog refinement.

## Purpose

Refinement makes Product Backlog items clearer, smaller, and better ordered. It is ongoing work, not a formal Scrum event.

## Good Refinement Outcomes

- Items near the top of the Product Backlog are understood well enough for planning.
- The Product Owner and Developers share enough context to discuss value, risk, and size.
- Large items are split before they block Sprint Planning.
- Acceptance examples, dependencies, and unknowns are visible.

## Workflow

1. Start with the Product Goal or near-term product outcome.
2. Review ordering: value, risk, learning, dependencies, and urgency.
3. Select the small set of items worth refining now.
4. Clarify user, need, outcome, constraints, and acceptance examples.
5. Split items that are too large or uncertain.
6. Identify open questions and decide who will resolve them.

## Product Owner Prompts

- What outcome makes this item valuable?
- What user or stakeholder need does it serve?
- What would make this item not worth doing?
- What decision do we need from Developers before ordering it?

## Developer Prompts

- What is unclear enough to affect planning?
- What technical risk should we learn about early?
- Can this be split vertically into a smaller user-visible outcome?
- What acceptance examples would prevent misunderstanding?

## Failure Modes

- Refining too far into the future.
- Turning refinement into design-by-committee.
- Treating estimates as the main purpose.
- Carrying vague items into Sprint Planning.
