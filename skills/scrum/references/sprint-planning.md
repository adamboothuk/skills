# Sprint Planning

Use this reference when the user wants help with Sprint Planning, Sprint Goals, or Sprint Backlog conversations.

## Purpose

Sprint Planning answers why the Sprint is valuable, what can be done, and how the selected work will get done.

## Inputs

- Product Goal.
- Product Backlog ordered by the Product Owner.
- Recent Increment and current product state.
- Team capacity, known absences, and constraints.
- Past performance and current Definition of Done.

## Workflow

1. Discuss why this Sprint matters.
2. Draft a Sprint Goal that describes a coherent objective, not just a list of items.
3. Select Product Backlog items that support the Sprint Goal.
4. Discuss feasibility with Developers.
5. Let Developers plan enough work to start and adapt.
6. Confirm the Sprint Backlog and visible Sprint Goal.

## Sprint Goal Qualities

A useful Sprint Goal is:

- Outcome-oriented.
- Coherent enough to guide trade-off decisions.
- Understandable to stakeholders.
- Flexible about exact scope where possible.
- Achievable within the Sprint timebox.

## Common Failure Modes

- Treating Sprint Planning as task assignment.
- Creating a Sprint Goal after selecting unrelated work.
- Overfilling the Sprint because every item seems important.
- Ignoring quality, integration, or Definition of Done work.
